I study how optimization and statistical principles can make machine learning methods more reliable and computationally effective. My work connects algorithm design and analysis with applications in networks, time series, and scientific data.

## Optimization and optimal transport

Gromov–Wasserstein learning compares data through their internal relationships, making it useful for graph alignment and learning across different domains. My work on **adaptive Bregman alternating projections** studies how to achieve feasibility and stationarity for the original constrained problem while retaining useful alignment performance.

[Adaptive Bregman Alternating Projections for Feasible Gromov-Wasserstein Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04264) · 2026 preprint; workshop papers accepted to GDDL and OPT at NeurIPS 2026.

## Structured inference for scientific data

Observed ecological interactions can miss genuine connections. I work on **sparse nonnegative factorization with imperfect detection** to recover both cross-group interactions and within-group similarity. This combines statistical modeling with nonconvex, nonsmooth optimization.

[Sparse Network Inference under Imperfect Detection and its Application to Ecological Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18820) · 2026 preprint; workshop paper accepted to TCCL at NeurIPS 2026.

## Statistical evaluation of generative models

Evaluating generated time series requires accounting for temporal dependence. Our **RENAL goodness-of-fit test** uses recurrent neural representations and statistical testing to assess whether a model captures the dynamics of observed sequences.

[Recurrent Neural Goodness-of-Fit Test for Time Series](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/zhang25i.html) · AISTATS 2025.

## Computational approaches to behavioral science

I have also worked on harmonizing large reaching datasets to study how age, sex/gender, and experience relate to motor control. This work uses statistical modeling to connect behavioral observations across studies.

[Large reaching datasets quantify the impact of age, sex/gender, and experience on motor control](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00383-7) · Communications Psychology, 2026.
